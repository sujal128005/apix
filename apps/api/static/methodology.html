APIx does not invent an index formula. It implements the formulae MoSPI specifies for CPI 2024, sourced from the Expert Group Report on Comprehensive Updation of the Consumer Price Index (January 2026, Price Statistics Division, NSO), applied at higher frequency and finer route granularity.

How a fare becomes an index number

1. Elementary level — Jevons short (chain-base). Within one route, one advance-purchase window, one day, the index chains onto its predecessor by the geometric mean of matched price relatives:

Iₜ = GM( pₜ / pₜ₋₁ ) × Iₜ₋₁

Matched pairs only. A pair is the same (carrier, flight number, fare brand) in both periods — comparing "cheapest today" with "cheapest yesterday" would record a change of airline as a change of price.

Product specification. A matched pair requires the same product, defined by the characteristics that determine its price: route, carrier, cabin, trip type, routing, departure-time band, fare brand, baggage entitlement, refundability, changeability and passenger type.

Flight number is deliberately excluded. It is operational metadata, not a price-determining characteristic: a schedule change that renumbers a flight leaves the product unchanged, and treating it as a new product would lose the matched pair and record no movement where there was one. Departure-time band is included instead — a dawn departure and a late-evening one are genuinely different products at genuinely different prices.

2. Route level — weighted arithmetic mean across the six advance-purchase windows.

3. Headline — Young / Modified Laspeyres, the weighted arithmetic mean of route indices. Weights come from a period earlier than the prices, which is why this is a Young index rather than a true Laspeyres.

Geometric below, arithmetic above. That asymmetry is deliberate CPI structure, not an inconsistency: the Expert Group Report records that CPI 2024 moved the house-rent index from weighted geometric to weighted arithmetic specifically to align it with every other item.

Parameters in force

Every index value stores the methodology version and weight-set version that produced it. A revision creates a new version and recomputes forward; a published value can never be silently overwritten, so a revision is never mistaken for a correction.

Precision

Each elementary index carries a sampling standard error, derived from the dispersion of log price relatives within the stratum (se = s / √n), with a Student's t multiplier below eight observations.

This is sampling error only. It measures how much the index would move had a different set of flights been observed within the same stratum. It does not capture basket, weighting or coverage error — which are larger sources here — and an interval presented as though it bounded total error would overstate what is known.

Known limitations

LimitationConsequence

APIx uses its own base period, not 2024 = 100 Index levels are not comparable with CPI. Only movements are, and only movements are ever reported
Coverage: economy, one-way, direct, single adult, 20 routes Narrower than the household consumption basket
